Course Overview

A computational view of printing beyond planar slicing

The course introduces computational fabrication methods for multi-axis and non-planar 3D printing, emphasizing how geometric and physical objectives become manufacturable layers, executable toolpaths, and verified machine motion.

It connects robotic curved-layer printing with constrained three-axis non-planar FFF, fiber-reinforced materials, inverse kinematics, collision-aware planning, and structure-fabrication co-optimization.

Technical Topics

From fields to executable fabrication

Scalar Fields and Support-Free Planning

Curved layers arise as iso-values of scalar fields, allowing planners to connect layer thickness, surface following, support reduction, and manufacturable deposition directions.

Reinforced FDM and Fiber-Aware Deposition

Path direction becomes a mechanical design variable when filament alignment and continuous fiber reinforcement affect anisotropic strength and structural performance.

S3-Slicer, Neural Slicer, and Co-Optimization

Deformation-based maps, rotation and scale fields, neural slicing fields, and topology-aware objectives connect layer generation with structure-fabrication co-design.

Collision-Free Planning and Toolpaths

A planned path must remain accessible, avoid collisions, satisfy clearance constraints, and transition into continuous toolpaths and inverse-kinematics-feasible machine motion.

Five-Axis Planning and IK

Hands-on examples compare collision-aware and semi-continuous toolpaths, then refine tool orientations, kinematic redundancy, and waypoint timing.

Three-Axis Non-Planar Practice

Fixed-nozzle printers cannot rotate the tool, but can still use surface-following paths to improve top surfaces, reduce stair-stepping, and explore limited material-orientation control.

Limits and Open Problems

What still needs better computational tools

  • Stable multi-objective optimization with interpretable trade-offs.
  • Semi-continuous paths that handle symmetry, singularities, and branch cuts.
  • Continuous carbon-fiber paths with manufacturable turning, anchoring, and overlap behavior.
  • Tighter coupling between fabrication planning, inverse kinematics, calibration, and controller limits.
  • Fast digital twins for online state estimation, collision updates, and toolpath correction.
  • Learning-driven planning methods that remain verifiable under hard fabrication constraints.
  • Reliable diagnostics and fallbacks when a model cannot be printed as planned.
